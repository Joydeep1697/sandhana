AI Interpretation

High Confidence
What happened?

Across 12 simulated launches, increasing aerodynamic drag coefficient reduced peak range by 18.4% and depressed the optimal launch angle from $45.0^\circ$ down to $41.8^\circ$ for standard projectiles.

Why did it happen?

Kinetic energy dissipation scales with velocity cubed ($P_{loss} \propto v^3$). High initial launch velocities experience maximum deceleration immediately after launch. Shallower trajectories spend less cumulative flight time traversing high-resistance low-altitude air.

✓ Supported by Current Model Consistent with Navier-Stokes
What remains uncertain?

Boundary-layer transition turbulence at varying Mach numbers and spin-induced Magnus lift forces ($\vec{\omega} \times \vec{v}$) are omitted in this pure planar formulation.

Recommended Inquiries:
  1. Test Magnus effect with angular spin vector.
  2. Sweep altitude barometric gradient $\rho(y) = \rho_0 e^{-y/H}$.
