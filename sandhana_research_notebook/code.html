Investigating Projectile Range with Non-linear Air Resistance

AS Dr. A. Sharma
• Last edited 12 minutes ago • Version 1.4
Peer-Review Ready
01

Research Question & Motivation

SEC_ID: 104.1

How does the mathematical transition from linear Stokes drag to Newtonian quadratic velocity-dependent drag shift the optimal ballistic launch angle away from the classical parabolic θ = 45°, and what are the empirical bounds on horizontal range damping across variable projectile masses?

lightbulb
Physical Rationale In real-world ballistics at Reynolds numbers Re > 1000, quadratic turbulent boundary layer separation dominates over laminar viscous drag. Classical textbook vacuum symmetry breaks down completely.
02

Hypothesis

H₁ HYPOTHESIS TESTED
“Because quadratic velocity damping (F_d ∝ v²) acts most intensely during early ascent where velocity reaches its global maximum v_0, vertical momentum decays faster than horizontal velocity. Consequently, optimal launch angle θ_{opt} will shift downwards to the range of approximately 40°–42° for smooth spherical projectiles in standard tropospheric air.”

Mathematical Formulation & Numerical Integration

ODE SYSTEM

The coupled system of second-order non-linear differential equations governing two-dimensional ballistic flight under isotropic air density ρ is given by Newton’s second law with quadratic drag resistance:

SYSTEM EQNS (VECTOR EXPANSION) INDEPENDENT VARIABLE: t
[Eq. 1] m · (dv_x / dt) = - ½ · C_d · ρ · A · v · v_x
[Eq. 2] m · (dv_y / dt) = - m · g - ½ · C_d · ρ · A · v · v_y
[Aux] where speed modulus: v = √(v_x² + v_y²)

Numerical Solution Strategy

Because of the non-linear velocity term v · v_i = v_i √(v_x² + v_y²), closed-form analytic solutions in terms of elementary functions do not exist for the terminal trajectory coordinates. We integrate iteratively using standard Runge-Kutta 4th Order (RK4) with adaptive time-step sizing:

Step Invariance Step size capped at Δt = 0.002 s guarantees truncation error Œ(h&sup5) < 10&supmin&sup6.
Ground Boundary Collision Bisection interpolation resolves impact time t_land precisely where y(t) = 0.
Energy Conservation Check Energy dissipation matches drag work ∫ F_d · v dt within 0.04% discrepancy.
